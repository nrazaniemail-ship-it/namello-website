KNOWLEDGE, EXPERIENCE & PSYCHOLOGY

Lessons from three distinct trading approaches

RISK MANAGEMENT · MACRO TRADING

پل تودور جونز / Paul Tudor Jones

In his interview in Jack Schwager’s Market Wizards, Paul Tudor Jones discusses protecting capital, defining an exit before entry, and viewing risk across the portfolio. The practical lesson: define the loss scenario and trade invalidation point before focusing on potential profit.

Namello exercise: record the stop, cash risk and exposure from other open positions for every trade.

SYSTEMATIC · TREND FOLLOWING

ریچارد دنیس / Richard Dennis

The 1980s Turtle experiment trained a group of novices to trade using a rule-based framework. Its documented approach included breakout entries, predefined exits and position sizing adjusted for market volatility. The lesson is disciplined rule execution—not blindly copying a historical system.

Namello exercise: write down your entry and exit rules, then grade how closely each trade followed them.
Explore the documented Turtle rules
PSYCHOLOGY · PROBABILISTIC THINKING

مارک داگلاس / Mark Douglas

Mark Douglas, author of Trading in the Zone, emphasizes that having a statistical edge does not mean being right on every trade. Even a sound method can experience losing streaks. In his interview with Larry Connors, keeping a journal and grading trades are discussed as ways to build trading self-awareness.

Namello exercise: grade execution separately from profit or loss; do not confuse one trade’s outcome with decision quality.

TRADING GLOSSARY

Build your trading vocabulary

۱۶ اصطلاح

These are concise educational definitions. Exact meanings can vary by market, instrument and broker.

Try: drawdown, spread, expectancy

Balance — Account balance

Account value based on closed trades and booked entries; it generally excludes floating P/L on open positions.

Equity — Live account value

Balance plus the floating profit or loss of open positions; it changes as the market moves.

Drawdown — Peak-to-trough decline

The decline from a previous equity peak to a subsequent low; magnitude and duration are both useful.

R-multiple — Outcome in risk units

Trade profit or loss divided by initial risk. A gain equal to twice the initial risk is +2R.

Risk/Reward — Risk-to-reward

Compares potential loss to the stop with potential gain to the target; it does not show the probability of success.

Expectancy — Statistical expectation

Average expected outcome per trade across a sample, commonly estimated from win rate, average win and average loss.

Win Rate — Winning-trade percentage

The percentage of profitable trades; without the size of wins and losses, it is not a complete performance measure.

Profit Factor — Gross profit / gross loss

Gross profits divided by gross losses; costs and data quality affect its interpretation.

Slippage — Execution price difference

The difference between the expected order price and actual execution price; it can widen in fast or illiquid markets.

Spread — Bid/ask difference

The difference between bid and ask prices; an implicit cost of entering and exiting a trade.

Commission — Trading fee

A fee charged by a broker or venue for execution or holding a position; fee structures vary.

Swap — Overnight financing

A charge or credit for holding certain positions past the daily rollover; it depends on instrument, direction and broker.

Margin — Required collateral

Funds set aside to maintain a position with a broker; it is not the same as the trade's loss.

Leverage — Exposure multiplier

Allows control of exposure larger than posted margin; it magnifies both gains and losses relative to equity.

ATR — Average True Range

A volatility measure based on true price range; it does not identify market direction by itself.

Breakout — Range breakout

A price move beyond an observable level or range; false breakouts occur, and confirmation depends on the system's rules.
