How to Analyze a MetaTrader 5 Trading Statement

An MT5 trading statement is a record of account activity—not a complete explanation of trading skill. Learn how to check the data, interpret the results and identify what needs further investigation.

Updated 2026-11-15 · Educational guide · Not financial advice

What an MT5 statement can show

Depending on the report format, account history and broker configuration, a MetaTrader 5 statement may contain closed deals, orders, positions, balance operations, profit and loss, commissions, swaps, timestamps and account-level summaries. The exact fields and terminology can vary. Start by identifying the report period, account, deposit currency and whether the report includes all relevant activity.

Step 1: Check the source before calculating

  • Confirm the account and reporting period.
  • Check whether the report is based on deals, orders or a summarized account history.
  • Identify the account currency and the broker's symbol and contract specifications.
  • Check the time zone used in timestamps and align it with any external price data.
  • Look for deposits, withdrawals, credits or balance corrections that should not be treated as trading profit.
  • Confirm whether commission, swap and other charges are included in the displayed result.
Data quality comes first.

A missing history range, duplicate export, changed symbol specification or inconsistent time zone can distort derived metrics. Keep the original report and document any cleaning or exclusions.

Step 2: Separate trading results from cash flows

Deposits and withdrawals change account balance but are not trading performance. For performance analysis, distinguish external cash flows from realized trading profit and costs. If you compare equity across dates, account for cash flows rather than interpreting every balance increase as investment return.

Step 3: Review costs and execution

Inspect commissions, swaps and the effect of spread and slippage where the report or supporting data makes them available. A strategy that looks profitable before costs may have little or no net edge after costs. Compare costs by instrument, holding time and trade frequency.

Step 4: Examine risk as well as return

  • Net profit: result after the costs included in the source data.
  • Profit factor: gross profit divided by the absolute value of gross loss.
  • Maximum drawdown: the largest peak-to-trough decline in the chosen balance or equity series.
  • Expectancy: average expected result per trade over the sample.
  • R-multiple: outcome relative to the initial risk, if that initial risk is known reliably.

Do not infer risk per trade from final profit alone. If stop distance, position size or initial risk is absent, an R-multiple may not be reconstructable without additional data.

Step 5: Segment the history

Compare results by symbol, strategy, direction, session and time period when those labels are available or can be matched reliably. Look for concentration: a small number of trades or instruments may account for most of the profit. Also inspect losing streaks and the distribution of outcomes, not just averages.

What a statement cannot tell you by itself

A statement usually cannot explain why a trade was taken, whether the setup followed your rules, what the trader was thinking, or whether a stop was moved. It may not contain enough price history to calculate MFE and MAE accurately. Those questions require a journal, trade annotations and—where needed—historical market data.

Use the original MT5 report as the source of truth, review any extracted values, and investigate discrepancies before drawing conclusions. Do not assume that every broker-specific field is interpreted identically.

Final verification checklist

  • Correct account, currency and date range
  • Complete history and no duplicate records
  • Deposits and withdrawals separated from trading P/L
  • Costs included consistently
  • Time zone and symbol specifications checked
  • Derived metrics traceable to source records

A careful statement review turns raw account history into evidence. It does not guarantee that past results will continue.
